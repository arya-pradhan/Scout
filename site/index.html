How it works

Photon carries the conversation. TinyFish does the web work.

Claude reads each text, picks from 18 tools, and only states what came back from the live web, with links.

The live webcourse sites · events · job boards
  1. 01

    Search

    "Find it" for a course's website or your school's events page, a job-search watch on recent results only, and research answers with sources.

    For when Scout doesn't have a URL yet, or the postings are spread across the whole web.

  2. 02

    Fetch

    Crawls a professor's site: the page plus its linked Calendar, Deliverables and Syllabus pages and PDFs, batched. Also events pages at schools without an API, and job boards.

    These pages are messy, JS-rendered or PDFs. Fetch returns clean text and links.

  3. 03

    Agent

    Confirms a posting is still open before Scout texts you, reads real application forms (questions, documents, deadline), and browses JS-heavy course sites and boards.

    Some answers take clicking through a live site. Every goal is read-only.

Photon Spectrum the iMessage layer

A real iMessage number, texts Scout sends first (briefs, nudges, alerts, reminders), tapbacks as controls, typing indicators, confetti and fireworks, rich links and a contact card.

Clean data stays clean no browser needed

Canvas calendar feeds, Engage and Localist event APIs, Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby job APIs, and the Simplify list are read directly. TinyFish handles the messy web.

Built to be trusted

A friend who texts you useful things, not a bot acting behind your back.

Read-only on the web.

Agent goals forbid signing in, typing into forms, applying or submitting. Scout can't book, buy, RSVP or email anything.

You approve every add.

Calendar links open pre-filled and you tap Save. Essay drafts only happen when you say yes.

Never fakes a source.

Deadlines, events and postings come only from real pages, each with its link. Items without a real date are dropped.

Quiet by default.

Deduped alerts, quiet hours, and no brief on days with nothing to say. When a site fails, Scout names it instead of hiding it.
